What we learned

  • SLH-DSA "s" signing is the real cost. ML-DSA works in micro- to milliseconds; SLH-DSA "s" sets take 0.5–4 s per signature in compiled code and 14–26 s in pure JavaScript.
  • liboqs leads ML-DSA by 2.5–10× thanks to hand-written AVX2. OpenSSL leads the SHAKE SLH-DSA sets, liboqs the SHA2 ones.
  • Hashing tricks were the biggest wins. Caching the SHA-2 state of the public seed gave 1.2–4× on SHA2 SLH-DSA; 4-lane AVX2 Keccak made RustCrypto's SHAKE SLH-DSA verify 4–5× faster.
  • noble's rewritten hot paths gave about 2× across all processes. That is a patched copy, not the npm release.
  • Build flags move little where assembly already exists; removing the assembly costs 20–40%.
  • Batch threads scale (2.2–3.5× on 4 cores); splitting one verify across threads does not. ML-DSA stages are too short for the hand-off.
  • Stack: most implementations need 1–8 KiB; RustCrypto ML-DSA keygen needs 197–428 KiB and liboqs ML-DSA 30–113 KiB.
